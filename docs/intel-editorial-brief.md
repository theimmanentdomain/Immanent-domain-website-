## Research and selection

Before preparing each edition:

1. Review accessible previous editions and identify what has already been covered.
2. Search current sources across all five subjects.
3. Prioritize substantive, undercovered developments and genuinely useful discoveries.
4. Include newly discovered older material when worthwhile, but clearly state its original date and do not present it as breaking news.
5. Repeat an item only when there is a meaningful development, correction, or newly verified evidence. Explain what changed.
6. Avoid counting multiple articles about the same development as separate discoveries.
7. Search thoroughly for five worthwhile entries in every section. If a section remains short, state the shortfall plainly. Never fabricate sources or add irrelevant material to reach 25.

Prefer original documents, research papers, organizer announcements, authoritative reporting, and credible independent analysts. Cross-check consequential conflict claims and reports circulating on social media.

Use only sources actually accessed. Never fabricate private access, verification, quotations, or source links.

## Format for each entry

Include:

- **Title.**
- **Direct source link**, with additional corroborating links when useful.
- **Publication or update date**, and the event date separately when relevant.
- **The development:** what happened, changed, or was discovered.
- **Significance:** why it matters.
- **Practical implications:** useful consequences or opportunities for IMDO and its readers, where supported.
- **Uncertainty:** a brief qualification when evidence is disputed, incomplete, or limited.

For events and opportunities, include available actionable details: date, time and timezone, venue, admission cost, ticket or application link, and deadline. Mark missing information as unconfirmed rather than guessing.

Distinguish verified facts, attributed claims, research results, practitioner claims, symbolic interpretations, and your own analytical or design inferences. Use explicit labels when the distinction materially affects the reader’s understanding.
